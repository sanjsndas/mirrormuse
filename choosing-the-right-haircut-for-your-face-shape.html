Choosing the Right Haircut for Your Face Shape

A haircut can change how your features are framed, but face shape is only one part of choosing a style. Hair texture, density, styling habits and personal taste matter just as much. Use these suggestions as a starting point, then discuss what will work for you with your stylist at Mirror Muse.

Start with your overall outline

Face-shape guides often group faces into oval, round, square, heart-shaped, diamond or oblong categories. In reality, many people have a blend of features, and it can be difficult to identify one shape with certainty. Rather than treating a label as a rule, notice which features you would like to highlight or soften.

You can look at your face in a mirror with your hair pulled back and consider the proportions of your forehead, cheekbones and jaw. A stylist can help assess your features and suggest cuts that suit your preferences.

Ideas to discuss with your stylist

Oval faces

An oval face is often considered versatile because its proportions can work with a wide range of lengths and shapes. You might consider a bob, long layers, a fringe or a close crop. Think about how much time you want to spend styling and whether you prefer hair that sits around your face or is swept away from it.

Round faces

If you would like to add definition or create a longer-looking outline, ask about styles with height at the crown, longer layers or an off-centre parting. A cut with movement around the jaw can also be worth exploring. These are options, not rules: a blunt bob or fringe may still be the right choice if you love the look.

Square faces

For a softer frame, you could discuss layers, a side-swept fringe or a shape with movement around the jaw. If you prefer a more defined look, a sharp bob or a clean, one-length cut may suit your style. Consider how the haircut will sit when your hair is at its natural texture.

Heart-shaped faces

A cut with some fullness around the jaw can balance a wider forehead, while side-swept or curtain-style fringes can change how the upper part of the face is framed. Chin-length styles and longer layers are possibilities to discuss, depending on your hair and preferred length.

Diamond-shaped faces

Layers or a fringe can help change the way the forehead and cheekbones are framed. You might also consider a bob or a cut with volume near the chin. Ask your stylist how the shape will look from the front and in profile, as well as how easily it can be styled at home.

Oblong faces

If you want to create the impression of width, consider a fringe, waves or layers that add movement at the sides. A shorter cut can also work well, depending on your features and taste. If you like long, straight hair, there is no need to avoid it; your stylist can help find a shape that feels balanced to you.

Consider your hair and daily routine

A haircut that looks appealing in a photo may behave differently with your hair’s natural texture, growth patterns and density. Bring reference images if you have them, but be ready to discuss what you like about each one—perhaps the length, fringe or overall shape—rather than expecting an exact match.

Also be honest about your routine. Consider how often you are willing to style your hair, whether you use heat tools and how frequently you want to visit the salon for a trim. A good cut should feel manageable between appointments, not just look polished on the day.

Make the choice together

Face-shape advice can help you explore options, but it should not limit them. The best haircut is one that suits your hair, fits your lifestyle and makes you feel comfortable. Tell your stylist what you want to keep, what you would like to change and how much styling you are happy to do. Together, you can choose a shape that feels like you.
